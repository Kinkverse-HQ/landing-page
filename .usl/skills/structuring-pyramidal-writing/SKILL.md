# Structuring Pyramidal Writing

Use the pyramid as a test of the thinking, not merely as a document template. Build one governing answer, derive each higher-level claim from its support, and let the audience's question determine what belongs.

## Frame the communication

Identify the decision or problem, the audience, what they already know, what they care about, and what the communication should change. State:

- **Subject:** a specific, contestable thesis rather than a topic label;
- **Question:** the main question that thesis raises for this audience;
- **Answer:** the shortest complete sentence the available evidence can support.

Do not force the user's preferred conclusion when the evidence points elsewhere. Mark an answer as provisional when sources, assumptions, or unresolved disagreement make certainty unwarranted. Ask for clarification only when the missing audience, decision, or evidence would materially change the pyramid.

## Build the pyramid

1. Inventory candidate claims, evidence, facts, constraints, and counterevidence. Keep observations separate from inferences.
2. Select the few supporting claims that together answer the governing question. Do not force three branches; use the number the argument needs.
3. Make every parent a substantive synthesis of its children, not a label such as "benefits" or "considerations." Reading downward should answer the question raised by the parent; reading upward should justify the parent.
4. At each horizontal level, group ideas of the same logical kind. Minimize overlap and cover what the answer requires, but do not manufacture artificial categories merely to claim perfect MECE coverage.
5. Give each group one defensible order: a deductive sequence, chronology, structural decomposition, causal chain, or ranked importance. Do not call a list deductive unless later points follow from earlier premises.
6. Stress-test whether each branch directly supports the answer, each item appears once for a reason, contrary evidence is handled, and the proposed action follows from the logic.

## Draft for the audience

Open with only the context needed to establish the situation, the change or tension, the audience's question, and the answer. Then follow the supporting claims in pyramid order. Start sections and paragraphs with their governing insight, place evidence beneath the claim it supports, and use informative headings when the medium benefits from them.

Preserve required format, voice, terminology, and uncertainty. Answer-first does not mean context-free: a skeptical or unfamiliar audience may need a short shared starting point before the answer. Narrative, exploratory, legal, scientific, or culturally specific forms may require a different surface order even when a pyramid helps test the underlying logic.

## Visible reasoning

Do the structural work without exposing private chain-of-thought. By default, return the finished communication and only the assumptions or caveats the reader needs.

When the user requests the full reasoning, an explicit pyramid, or the supplied long-form workflow, read [the full pyramid format](references/full-pyramid-format.md). Provide a reviewable rationale, evidence map, and uncertainty—not hidden token-by-token deliberation.

## Output contract

Deliver a communication with one identifiable governing answer, non-overlapping support that actually entails or substantiates it, and evidence placed at the correct level. If the evidence cannot sustain a clean pyramid, expose the gap instead of filling it with invented certainty or padded prose.
