# Full pyramid format

Use this format when the user asks to see the planning artifact as well as the final communication. Adapt labels to the medium, but retain the distinctions between framing, support, hierarchy, and draft.

Do not disclose private chain-of-thought. Each analytical prose section below must contain at least five substantive sentences that communicate conclusions, evidence, assumptions, alternatives, or uncertainty. Do not pad a section with restatement; if the evidence is thin, use the space to identify what is unknown and how it affects the answer. The final draft follows the requested length and medium rather than a sentence quota.

## Problem statement and subject

In at least five sentences, define the problem or decision, the proposed thesis, the audience, the relevant audience context, and the change the communication should produce. State the main question the thesis will trigger. End with the answer as one clear sentence. A single word is acceptable only when it truly carries the claim; most useful answers need a complete sentence.

## Reasoning summary

In at least five sentences, explain why the answer resolves the governing question. State the logical path at a reviewable level, identify decisive evidence, distinguish fact from inference, acknowledge meaningful counterevidence, and calibrate confidence. This is a concise rationale, not a transcript of internal deliberation.

## Supporting arguments

In at least five sentences, explain which candidate arguments were selected, why they are necessary, how they jointly support the answer, and which tempting but irrelevant or redundant points were excluded. Then list the chosen supporting claims and their evidence. Use complete, assertive sentences for claims; use facts, data, observations, or explicit logical implications for evidence.

## Pyramid layers

In at least five sentences, explain the grouping rule, the relationship between parent and child claims, the ordering rule within each group, remaining overlaps or gaps, and any branch that depends on an assumption. Then render the pyramid. Use as many branches and levels as the argument requires rather than filling a fixed three-by-three grid.

```text
Subject: <specific thesis>
Question: <the audience's governing question>
Answer: <one concise, evidence-supported sentence>

1. <supporting claim>
   1.1 <evidence or subordinate claim>
   1.2 <evidence or subordinate claim>
2. <supporting claim>
   2.1 <evidence or subordinate claim>
   2.2 <evidence or subordinate claim>
```

## Draft

Write the final communication in the user's requested format. The introduction establishes only the shared context and tension needed to make the question relevant, then gives the answer. Each following section begins with a supporting claim and develops its evidence. Keep the surface concise even though the planning artifact is thorough.
